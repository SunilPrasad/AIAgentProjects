# Aryabhata: A Life of Numbers and Stars

Aryabhata was an Indian mathematician and astronomer whose work helped people understand numbers, the Earth and the sky. He was born in 476 CE, during an age when art, science and learning were flourishing in India. His achievements would influence scholars for many centuries.

Aryabhata worked in Kusumapura, an important centre of learning. There, he brought together mathematics and the study of the heavens. His work dealt with large numbers and circles, the movement of the Earth, and the causes of eclipses. These were difficult subjects, but he expressed many of his ideas in a remarkably compact book.

## A Young Scholar's Achievement

In 499 CE, when he was just twenty-three, Aryabhata completed his best-known work, the *Aryabhatiya*. It became one of the lasting achievements of his life.

The book was written in verse. Instead of filling long chapters with explanations, Aryabhata put mathematical rules and astronomical ideas into short lines. Those lines contained a great deal of knowledge. Later scholars studied them closely and wrote explanations to help other readers understand his methods.

The *Aryabhatiya* covered several connected subjects. Some sections dealt with mathematics, while others explained calculations involving time and the heavens. Aryabhata included methods for working with square and cube roots, as well as ideas about circles and the movement of celestial bodies—the objects in the sky.

His work showed how mathematics could help astronomers. Careful calculations offered a way to describe patterns, compare measurements and understand changes that appeared in the heavens. Numbers were tools for studying the world.

Aryabhata also used letters of the Indian alphabet to represent numbers. His system could express very large quantities through combinations of letters. It was a different way of recording calculations, suited to the mathematical writing in his work.

## New Ways to Understand the World

One of Aryabhata's important mathematical results concerned circles. He gave a value of 3.1416 for pi, the number that connects the distance around a circle with the distance across its centre. His approximation was remarkably accurate.

This achievement was more than a collection of digits. It helped show how a familiar shape could be described through a precise mathematical relationship. Aryabhata's work brought together rules and calculations that other mathematicians could study and use.

His ideas about the Earth were equally striking. Aryabhata stated that the Earth rotates on its own axis, an imaginary line through its poles. He used this movement to explain why the stars appear to travel across the sky. The apparent motion of the stars could be understood through the movement of the Earth itself.

The idea was not widely accepted in his time. It formed an important part of his contribution to astronomy and became a subject for later scholars to examine and discuss.

Aryabhata also explained eclipses through the behaviour of light and shadow. A lunar eclipse happens when the Earth blocks sunlight from reaching the Moon. A solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes between the Earth and the Sun, blocking our view of the Sun.

These explanations described events that could look dramatic and mysterious. Aryabhata showed how they could be understood through the positions of the Earth, Sun and Moon. He also recognised that the Moon and planets shine by reflecting sunlight.

Together, his mathematical and astronomical work offered ways to explain the world through calculations and physical causes. His book connected the study of numbers with some of the most impressive sights in the sky.

## A Lasting Influence

Aryabhata's work continued to matter long after his own lifetime. Indian mathematicians and astronomers studied his methods, discussed his ideas and developed the subjects further. His writing became part of a continuing tradition of learning.

Through translations, his work also reached scholars beyond India, including in the Islamic world. Mathematical rules and astronomical ideas could cross languages and travel between places. Later readers could build on knowledge recorded centuries before them.

The *Aryabhatiya* remains central to Aryabhata's legacy. Written when he was a young man, it preserved ideas that continued to attract the attention of scholars across generations. His work on mathematics, Earth's rotation and eclipses earned him a lasting place in the history of science.

Aryabhata's life is remembered through these achievements: a compact book of verse, powerful methods of calculation and explanations that helped people understand the heavens. His contribution became part of India's rich mathematical heritage and the wider story of learning around the world.
